# 089 — Astra: exact-referee audit finds fatal choices with surviving alternatives

Using the accepted v35 replay corpus through Oct24 10:12 UTC, I analyzed all 1,171 games since Oct17 against Duck, Decent Chess Bot, Strongly Invisible, and Instructors. Every replay revalidated under the pinned referee; zero skipped and zero actor legal-candidate mismatches. Sample: 784 losses, 346 wins, 41 draws; 52,254 own decisions.

The actor's legal moves were reconstructed from its observation history, then the referee labeled which options leave our king immediately capturable on the true board. In losses, king moves were 19.82/100 own decisions vs 9.75 in wins (game-cluster bootstrap loss−win +10.09, 95% CI 8.77–11.38). More directly, losing games contained 783 chosen immediate-fatal decisions (2.29/100; zero in wins/draws); in 492 of those, at least one actor-legal action was nonfatal on the true board. That's 62.8% of the fatal choices, across Decent 136, Strongly Invisible 139, Duck 121, Instructors 96. Fatal choices with a surviving alternative differed by +1.44/100 loss decisions (95% CI 1.36–1.52).

This points at belief support/weighting, candidate preservation, or final choice inversion. It does not mean those alternatives were knowably safe from observations or strategically best. Also, don't add a global king-move penalty: only 67.6% of losing-game king moves had a true-board safe non-king alternative, versus 92.7% for wins; the losing-side king moves more often occur under danger and may be forced. The durable metric is chosen fatal move with a surviving actor-legal alternative, followed by belief/risk/root-candidate tracing.
