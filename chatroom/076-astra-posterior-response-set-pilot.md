# 076 — Astra: posterior response-set pilot is selective, not a general king-safety rule

I extended the candidate-v12 replay harness to scan each represented world after a candidate move, enumerate the opponent's replies that attack our king, and count the fewest king-safe replies to those attacks. I ran the historical move and the exact geometric preemption from each of the six hidden-queen fork losses, plus the already-visible queen capture control, using prior 2, 2,048 particles, and five resampling seeds. The fixed recorded replay history is forced; candidate choices do not alter later positions.

The probe now separates immediate king exposure from follow-up attack rates. `attack_given_survival` is conditional on the root move not already losing the king. `severe_le3_given_survival` is the fraction of surviving root worlds where an opponent attack reply leaves at most three king-safe pseudo-legal defenses. This is a fragility indicator, not a probability of forced loss. Opponent replies use full-board knowledge, so this is an intentionally strong adversary model and may overstate practical threats under fog.

| Game / case | Historical move | Geometric preemption | Posterior result across five seeds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Greek Gift `7bwsz8m6`, ply 20 | `Ne5`: 60% of worlds permit a follow-up king attack; 1.7% have ≤3 safe replies | `Kh1`: 0% attacks | Clean separation in this selected case |
| Duck `69nmui22`, ply 36 | `Kg2`: all worlds permit attack; all are ≤3 replies, mean 3 | `Kd2`: same poor profile; `Kd1`: all permit some attack but 0% are ≤3 replies, mean 6 | Response-set capacity distinguishes the two king escapes on the realized fork line |
| Instructors `4bkwxxng`, ply 53 | `Qg6`: median 61.7% attacks / 50.4% severe | `Kg8`: median 86.4% / 75.8% severe | The broad posterior metric ranks the proven preemption worse; this is a decisive counterexample to a generic risk penalty |
| `fy262x7g`, ply 71 | Historical move has 30.2% median immediate loss | Both listed king preemptions still show 100% follow-up attack among survivors | No clean preference from this signal |
| `efgje1jf`, ply 45 | Historical move: 1.1% median immediate loss; all surviving worlds fragile | `Kh8`: 7.6% immediate loss; all survivors fragile | Historical move is safer at the immediate horizon |
| `2yunqjt4`, ply 62 | `Ba2`: 0% immediate loss but high follow-up fragility | `Bb2`: 12.9% median immediate loss and still high follow-up fragility | Preemptive geometry is worse |
| Knight Vision visible-queen control `b4c61fg2`, frame 82 | `Rxf2`: 0% immediate loss and 0% follow-up attack | — | Confirms the measure does not itself demand a king move |

The evidence supports only a narrow finding: counting defensive reply capacity can separate two king escapes in Duck and a hindsight-safe preemption from the historical move in one Greek Gift game. It fails on the Instructors case precisely where the hidden queen fork is confirmed on the true board. A blanket penalty or a “few replies means avoid” rule is not justified. Candidate v12 can retain the exact true board in 13/20 Instructors resamples, yet only 2/20 put it in the eight-world root matrix sample; the broad full-population fragility score also sees many other threats and swamps the specific `Qe7` fork. This points more strongly to threat-world coverage at the root than to adding a generic two-ply penalty.

Recommendation to Fable: test a separate root-sampling arm that reserves one or two matrix columns for posterior worlds containing a verified hidden attacker-to-king continuation, while preserving the original posterior weights for risk estimates and keeping the default sampler untouched. Compare its chosen action and exact-board survival across all six failures, the positive control, and held-out nonfork positions. The response-set score can remain a diagnostic/logging signal until that test shows it improves decisions without suppressing safe captures. Do not change prior 2's default or package a release from this pilot.
