# 2 November review: what has learned, what has improved play

Astra · 2 November 2026, 11:10 UTC · For Henry and Fable's review

**We have useful new models and measurement tools, but no demonstrated Astra
playing improvement ready to replace v37.** The next investment should produce
better action choices, with data that measures those choices. Another model-size
sweep is not justified by the results below.

Fable's 11:01 UTC checkpoint records v37 at 138 wins, 9 draws and 127 losses
in 274 games: score 52.0%, Elo 1908.4, rank 11. Weak pairings include Crimson
Fog (1–0–11), duck (5–1–16) and Strongly Invisible (6–0–20). The overall score
resembles v35's 51.7%, but that comparison does not adjust for opponent mix,
version changes or scheduling. It is not a controlled proof of equivalence.
[Fable's checkpoint and refit record](https://github.com/htansetiawan/iamavirus/blob/main/fable-submissions/analysis/refit-all-20261101/result.md).

## Findings that should change our next decision

| Question | Evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Does much more data fix the existing move formula? | Fable refitted on 148,357 games versus 2,029. Full-model held-out improvement was about 0.008 nats; v37's 100-game local result was 56–2–42 with an interval spanning equality. | Keep collecting data, but do not promise a large gain from repeating this same refit. This does not rule out better representations or posterior objectives. |
| Can a model learn an opponent's observable responses? | The compact attention-distilled policy improved over its observable linear baseline by 0.141 nats. A persistent learned-opponent search mode subsequently scored 9–11 versus v35 and 10–1–9 versus v37 before a Misty control timeout closed confirmation. | Predictive improvement is real within its test; playing superiority is unproven. That baseline was not the deployed full-board prior. Do not restart the same integration as an untried idea. |
| Can we learn better hidden-board weights directly? | Matched posterior training improved held-out posterior NLL by 0.0205 nats; the move-trained control worsened it. Neither the original nor larger-capacity posterior fit met its frozen advancement thresholds. | Measure posterior quality, not only move accuracy. Small gains are not proof of no value, but cannot be promoted by changing the completed test's rules. |
| Can better risk probabilities improve decisions? | King-risk calibration improved held-out log loss from 0.07212 to 0.05887. Its subsequent search integrations failed their decision tests. The recovered recurrent risk head also failed its complementarity screen. | Calibration, ranking moves and selecting winning moves are distinct objectives. More calibration alone is not the next candidate. |
| Does a fog-trained frontier value help? | Compact native models are feasible. The bounded Stockfish residual improved pooled prediction but worsened both loss and Brier on the fresh-date cohort, failing confirmation. | Recorded final outcomes mix different policies and private histories. Do not substitute historical outcome prediction for evidence of better counterfactual action values. |
| Does faster code buy enough extra search? | Exact ordinary belief updates ran about 1.99 times faster. A whole-turn profile saved a median 7.3% of time but added only 1.8% more nodes. | Retain the implementation improvement; its kernel speed is not an Elo estimate. The time allocation and search/evaluation cost still matter. |

## What is running, and why

Experiment 244 asks whether we can obtain useful action comparisons from
restored private histories. Sixteen new source games contribute two proposed
actions and two seeds each: 64 continuations, with the same charged proposal
time and normal move budgets. Both continuations use fixed v37-derived policies.
It runs on two reserved core pairs with a five-hour cap; there is no GPU job,
model fitting or new ZIP. Its original launcher remains live at this review.

The output will measure natural endings, censored outcomes, repeat variability,
and time per independent source. The existing higher-root-value selector is a
descriptive baseline a learned selector would have to beat. A cutoff remains
unknown, with explicit return bounds; it is not scored as a draw. All branches
from one source stay together in any future split.

This is a controlled action intervention on one recorded hidden state per
source. It does not average over the full belief or reproduce duck's future
policy. Sixteen sources cannot justify training a new network. The previous
two-source pilot would extrapolate to roughly 112 hours for 1,000 sources on
two lanes, so cost is a substantive decision here, not bookkeeping.
[Frozen protocol](https://github.com/htansetiawan/iamavirus/blob/main/astra-submissions/experiments/action-data-panel-20261102/README.md)
and [learning requirements](https://github.com/htansetiawan/iamavirus/blob/main/astra-submissions/analysis/action-data-panel-20261102/learning-readiness.md).

## Proposed order after this review

1. **Finish and audit 244 once.** Produce a complete cost/signal report and the
   simple-selector comparison before choosing a training method. No retries or
   extra sources are added to rescue its outcome.
2. **Review one learning interface jointly.** The current proposal is a
   history-conditioned critic comparing the two supported root actions. The
   target is return under an explicitly named continuation policy. If 244
   supplies little reliable action contrast or costs too much, revise the
   teacher/data strategy before any bulk fit. A learned critic is not yet an
   approved experiment, a CFR value function, or a stronger bot.
3. **Require an opponent-transfer check before a release claim.** Complete
   games against fixed v35/v37 and an independent opponent can reveal different
   failures. Existing hybrid replicas did not pass fidelity checks; they cannot
   substantiate claims about Instructors or duck. Authentic arena results remain
   necessary for those claims.

Keep exact speed improvements and known rule/guard correctness repairs on a
separate engineering track. The queen-capture semantics fix has direct referee
evidence; its combined development screen failed, with a differing decision
where that predicate was not reached. Preserve both facts rather than calling
the bug correct or claiming a causal strength regression from that run.
[Queen audit](https://github.com/htansetiawan/iamavirus/blob/main/astra-submissions/analysis/queen-terminal-backport-20261102/README.md).

Fable is asked to reconcile this evidence with its proposed opponent-view and
per-move correction work, identify any materially different active experiment,
and help choose one next candidate with a fixed resource cap and next daily
review. This document records Astra's assessment, not committee agreement.
There is no new model launch, release reservation or deployment in this review.

## Update: the action-data panel finished

The 64 first-attempt branches completed in 127.1 minutes, with no technical
failure. The audit verifies 5,479 frames and 128 privacy checks. There are
50 terminal outcomes, 14 cutoffs, and only seven of 16 sources with all four
outcomes. Four of 19 fully observed same-action repeat pairs disagree. The
higher-root-value selector's advantage remains unresolved; no new policy is
promoted.

At this measured rate, 1,000 sources with four branches each cost about 123
hours on two lanes, before proposals. Do not scale that recipe or train a
large model on 16 sources. Existing search-score differences retain positive
empirical covariance with outcome differences under the missing-return
bounds, but this does not establish population correlation or a variance
saving. Fable has been asked to review that cheaper-teacher possibility and
its cost accounting. [Full results and audited dataset](https://github.com/htansetiawan/iamavirus/blob/main/astra-submissions/analysis/action-data-panel-20261102/README.md).

## Later update: archived neural king predictor does not clear the v37 screen

Astra experiment 248 completes a bounded 16-game, 890-decision comparison in
83 seconds, with no GPU or fitting. The archived v23 CNN alone is worse than
v37's hidden-king marginal. A fixed geometric pool improves overall NLL by
only 0.018 nats over the flattened baseline and worsens it after ply 30,
failing both the minimum improvement and later-game safeguard. Independent
output/label/metric audit passes. No integration or release follows; the old
hybrid-era neural advantage cannot be assumed to transfer to today's belief.
See [the completed report](../astra-submissions/analysis/hidden-king-complement-20261102/README.md).

## Later update: compact MultiPV imitation misses its accuracy target

Experiment 249 reuses 826,750 saved Stockfish action scores to train a 551KB
parent-encoding/move-query network. One 14-second GPU fit reduces error from
initialization but misses all frozen validation requirements: MAE 0.281
against a 0.08 limit; teacher regret 0.215 against 0.05; large-regret rate
14.6% against 1%. Independent scoring and checkpoint audits pass. The remaining
action-relative error cannot be fixed by a parent-wide calibration. There is
no native speed or playing-strength claim, and no integration follows.
[Result and reusable data](../astra-submissions/analysis/multipv-distillation-20261102/README.md).
